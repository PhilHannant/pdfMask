About pdfMask

Free PDF tools that do their work in your browser, so your documents never leave your device. Built and run by one person in the UK.

Who's behind it

I'm Phil Hannant, and I build and run pdfMask. There's no support desk or team in between: if you email, you reach me.

The site's code is public on GitHub, so anyone can read exactly what it does with a file.

Why it exists

Most online PDF tools work by uploading your document to their servers. That's a lot to ask when the document is a contract, a bank statement or a client's case file.

pdfMask takes the other approach. The tools load into your browser and do everything there. Nothing you open is uploaded, stored or logged, and the security page explains how you can check that for yourself.

It also takes redaction seriously. Drawing a black box over text doesn't remove it, and that mistake keeps exposing confidential details. The redactor removes the content itself, and the redaction checker finds text still hiding in files redacted elsewhere.

What it costs

The tools are free to use, with no sign-up, no adverts and no watermarks.

Contact

Questions, problems and suggestions are all welcome: hello@pdfmask.co.uk.

Please don't email confidential documents. If a file isn't working, describe what happens, or send a sample with made-up details.

Open-source software pdfMask uses

pdfMask is built on these open-source libraries, with thanks to the people who maintain them. Each is served from pdfmask.co.uk at a fixed version.

  • PDF.js by Mozilla, for reading and displaying PDFs (Apache License 2.0)
  • pdf-lib, for building and merging PDFs (MIT licence)
  • qpdf, for unlocking PDFs (Apache License 2.0), compiled for the browser by qpdf-wasm (ISC licence)
